Mapping clinical goniometry to **MediaPipe Pose’s 33-point topology** in a 2D single-camera image plane (mediapipe_2d_image_plane) requires three foundational geometric rules:

1. **Pixel-Space Conversion:** Never compute angles on raw [0, 1] normalized coordinates—multiply x by image width and y by image height first, otherwise non-square aspect ratios (e.g., 16:9 or 4:3) warp the angles by up to 8°–12°.

1. **Interior Angle vs. Clinical Neutral Zero ($0^\circ$):** A 3-point vector dot product $(P_{\text{proximal}} \to P_{\text{vertex}} \leftarrow P_{\text{distal}})$ yields $\approx 180^\circ$ when a limb is straight (or $\approx 90^\circ$ at the ankle). To match AAOS/AMA clinical goniometry ($0^\circ$ = neutral), apply the **Neutral Zero Formula** column.

1. **2D Image-Plane Limits (**NOT_MEASURABLE**):** Movements in the **Transverse plane** (axial rotations) or metrics requiring bony landmarks MediaPipe does not track (ASIS, PSIS, spinous processes, patellar borders) cannot be measured accurately from a standard 2D frontal/sagittal camera and are explicitly flagged below for your NOT_MEASURABLE catalog / Study A exclusions.

## **6. Reference Python Implementation (Pixel-Space + Visibility Gate)**
This drop-in pattern aligns with your services/ml-service architecture: it converts normalized coordinates to pixel space, checks per-metric landmark visibility (avoiding the whole-view occlusion bug noted in your plan), and supports both 3-point interior angles and horizontal/vertical reference lines.

Python

import math from typing import Literal, NamedTuple  class Point2D(NamedTuple):     x_px: float     y_px: float     visibility: float  def extract_pixel_point(     landmarks: list, idx: int, image_width: int, image_height: int ) -> Point2D:     lm = landmarks[idx]     return Point2D(         x_px=lm.x * image_width,         y_px=lm.y * image_height,         visibility=getattr(lm, "visibility", 1.0),     )  def compute_3pt_angle_deg(     proximal: Point2D,     vertex: Point2D,     distal: Point2D,     min_visibility: float = 0.65, ) -> float | None:     """     Returns the interior 2D image-plane angle (0 to 180 deg) at `vertex`,     or None if any required landmark falls below `min_visibility`.     """     if min(proximal.visibility, vertex.visibility, distal.visibility) < min_visibility:         return None      v1x, v1y = proximal.x_px - vertex.x_px, proximal.y_px - vertex.y_px     v2x, v2y = distal.x_px - vertex.x_px, distal.y_px - vertex.y_px      norm1 = math.hypot(v1x, v1y)     norm2 = math.hypot(v2x, v2y)     if norm1 < 1e-6 or norm2 < 1e-6:         return None      cos_theta = max(-1.0, min(1.0, (v1x * v2x + v1y * v2y) / (norm1 * norm2)))     return math.degrees(math.acos(cos_theta))

## **5. Key Physiological Concepts for Clinical & 2D Camera Assessment**
1. **Cyriax Physiological End-Feels:**

  1. **Soft (Soft Tissue Approximation):** Motion stops due to compression of muscle bulk (e.g., elbow flexion, knee flexion). In obese or highly muscular subjects, the maximum angle will be **5°–15° lower** than textbook norms without any joint pathology.

  1. **Firm (Capsular / Muscular / Ligamentous):** Motion stops with a springy or leathery resistance as ligaments, joint capsules, or muscle-tendon units reach elastic tension (most human joints).

  1. **Hard (Bony):** Motion stops abruptly when bone contacts bone (e.g., elbow extension).

1. **Active vs. Passive Range of Motion (AROM vs. PROM):**

  1. **AROM** (subject moves under their own muscle power—what a camera pipeline captures) is typically **3°–5° less** than **PROM** (clinician moves the limb with a goniometer to end-feel). When comparing 2D camera measurements against a physiotherapist's manual goniometry in a protocol like Study A, both measurements **must be matched as Active ROM** (or static hold at end-range), otherwise systematic negative bias will occur.

1. **Biarticular Muscle Passive Insufficiency:**

  1. Two-joint muscles (hamstrings, rectus femoris, gastrocnemius, biceps/triceps brachii) alter the maximum angle of one joint depending on the angle of the adjacent joint. Standardizing adjacent joint posture (e.g., knee flexed vs. knee extended during ankle dorsiflexion or hip flexion) is mandatory in any measurement protocol.

### **2. Resolving Edge Cases #1 & #4: Unbalanced Repeated-Measures LoA, Repeatability ($s_w$), and Sign-Flip Detection in** app/analysis/agreement.py
In agreement.py, solve **Edge Case #1** by implementing **both** "subject_means" and "bland_altman_2007_unbalanced" (the exact one-way ANOVA variance partitioning formula from Bland & Altman 2007 that works even when volunteers have unequal valid trial counts), plus a dedicated compute_within_subject_repeatability helper for the physiotherapist's duplicate measurements. Solve **Edge Case #4** by computing Pearson $r$ and emitting a probable_sign_inversion warning if $r$ is less than $-0.5$.

$$\sigma_{\text{total}}^2 = \sigma_{\text{between}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{within}}^2 = \frac{\text{MS}_{\text{subject}} - \text{MS}_{\text{error}}}{k_0} + \text{MS}_{\text{error}}, \quad \text{where } k_0 = \frac{1}{k - 1}\left(N - \frac{\sum m_i^2}{N}\right)$$
