§ 2 · INTERPRETATION

Clinical Synthesis & Key Findings

Non-SOAP format. Prepared from the supplied PosturePulse assessment and supplemental metric summaries.

Data reviewed  21/21 video-derived posture metrics; supplemental posture and bilateral thermal summaries

Clinical synthesis

The supplied measurements show small static alignment differences, most evident in sagittal trunk inclination and side-specific landmark offsets. Several frontal-plane findings overlap their stated reference once measurement uncertainty is considered. The bilateral trapezius and lumbar thermal differences are 0.0 C in the supplemental summary, indicating no measured side-to-side thermal asymmetry at that capture. These data do not establish a structural disorder, pain generator, neurologic deficit, vascular abnormality, or diagnosis.

Key findings

Trunk positionleft and right trunk inclination were -2.9 +/- 2.4 deg and -4.0 +/- 2.3 deg relative to the source system's 0-degree reference. Their uncertainty intervals remain below 0 degrees, supporting a small measured inclination during this recording.
Lateral translationlateral body shift was -1.2 +/- 1.0% of stature. Small in magnitude, but its stated interval does not cross the 0% reference.
Frontal alignmenthead tilt, shoulder-line tilt, hip-line tilt and trunk lateral lean each overlapped the source reference after uncertainty was applied; no definite frontal-plane deviation can be inferred.
Side-specific offsetsleft ear, shoulder and hip offsets were greater than corresponding right-side values. Geometric observations without source reference ranges; require correlation with stance, camera position and examination.
Kneesknee extension overlapped 0 degrees bilaterally. Knee frontal alignment was explicitly labeled a proxy, carried wide uncertainty, and had no source reference value.
Thermal summarytrapezius Delta T and lumbar Delta T were both +0.0 C against the supplied <0.5 C comparison criterion. Supports bilateral surface-temperature symmetry only under the conditions of that capture.

Data integrity & reconciliation

The primary PDF reports raw landmark geometry and uncertainty. The supplemental posture table reports transformed values against a 90-degree 'ideal'. The definitions, processing version and uncertainty for those transformed metrics are not provided; treat as a separate summary layer, not substituted for or averaged with the primary PDF values.

Important qualification

The labels 'textbook perfect', 'optimal neurovascular symmetry' and 'excellent' exceed what these measurements can establish. A 0.0 C side-to-side difference does not prove normal neurovascular function, and a single static posture value does not determine pathology or clinical risk.

§ 4 · IMPLICATIONS & PLAN

Clinical Implications & Management Framework

Translates the measured pattern into a safe clinical workflow. Not a diagnosis or treatment prescription.

Clinical significance

The pattern is best characterized as minor static postural asymmetry with small sagittal trunk inclination. Relevant frontal-plane uncertainty intervals overlap the source reference. Side-specific offsets may reflect stance, camera perspective, landmark localization, natural asymmetry or a reproducible strategy; significance depends on symptoms and repeatability. Equal bilateral trapezius and lumbar surface temperatures are neutral rather than diagnostic.

Recommended clinical follow-up

01Correlate with symptoms and functionRecord pain, aggravating tasks, neurologic symptoms, work exposure, tolerance and prior injury.
02Perform focused physical examinationAssess relaxed stance/gait, regional motion, landmarks, strength, sensation, reflexes and relevant provocation.
03Repeat under standardized conditionsMatch camera setup, stance, clothing, lighting and software; obtain at least two trials.
04Conservative movement planIf symptomatic, use position changes, ergonomic adjustment and graded mobility/strength work.
05Escalate when clinically indicatedPrompt assessment for progressive deficit, gait change, bowel/bladder or saddle symptoms, severe night pain, fever, trauma or systemic illness.

Suggested reassessment outcomes

Prioritize symptoms and task tolerance. Secondary outcomes are repeatable changes in trunk inclination and lateral shift beyond expected variability, supported by functional tests and standardized side-offset or thermal measurements.

Technical limitations

Single static video-derived observation; no direct assessment of bone, joint pathology, muscle activation or dynamic control. Several values are unreferenced proxies, not disease thresholds. The source is marked draft and not clinically validated. The supplemental posture convention lacks uncertainty and algorithm provenance; thermal data lack absolute temperatures and acquisition protocol.

Evidence note

Photogrammetry can provide repeatable quantitative posture measurements when acquisition and landmarks are standardized, but validity varies by method and metric. Thermal measurement reliability likewise depends on equipment and protocol; symmetry alone is not a diagnosis.

Conclusion

The available evidence supports documenting a small, non-acute static alignment pattern and bilateral thermal symmetry at one time point. It does not support a diagnosis, a claim of neurovascular normality, or a universal posture correction target. Clinical decisions should be driven by symptoms, examination, function and standardized repeat measurement.
