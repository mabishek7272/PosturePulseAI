  Clinical synthesis
  The supplied measurements show small static alignment differences, most evident in sagittal trunk inclination and
  side-specific landmark offsets. Several frontal-plane findings overlap their stated reference once measurement uncertainty is
  considered. The bilateral trapezius and lumbar thermal differences are 0.0 C in the supplemental summary, indicating no
  measured side-to-side thermal asymmetry at that capture. These data do not establish a structural disorder, pain generator,
  neurologic deficit, vascular abnormality, or diagnosis.


 Key findings
  • Trunk position: left and right trunk inclination were -2.9 +/- 2.4 degrees and -4.0 +/- 2.3 degrees relative to the source
    system's 0-degree reference. Their uncertainty intervals remain below 0 degrees, supporting a small measured inclination
    during this recording.

  • Lateral translation: lateral body shift was -1.2 +/- 1.0% of stature. This is small in magnitude, but its stated interval does
    not cross the 0% reference.

  • Frontal alignment: head tilt, shoulder-line tilt, hip-line tilt and trunk lateral lean each overlapped the source reference after
    uncertainty was applied; no definite frontal-plane deviation can be inferred from those individual measures.

  • Side-specific offsets: left ear, shoulder and hip offsets were greater than corresponding right-side values. These are
    geometric observations without source reference ranges and require correlation with stance, camera position and
    examination.

  • Knees: knee extension overlapped 0 degrees bilaterally. Knee frontal alignment was explicitly labeled a proxy, carried wide
    uncertainty, and had no source reference value.

  • Thermal summary: trapezius Delta T and lumbar Delta T were both +0.0 C against the supplied <0.5 C comparison
    criterion. This supports bilateral surface-temperature symmetry only under the conditions of that capture.

 Data integrity and reconciliation
 The primary PDF reports raw landmark geometry and uncertainty. The supplemental posture table reports transformed values
 against a 90-degree 'ideal' (forward head 80.7 degrees, shoulder elevation 87.4 degrees, pelvic tilt 87.0 degrees, trunk lean
 85.5 degrees, and knee values 1.9/2.2 degrees). The definitions, processing version and uncertainty for those transformed
 metrics are not provided. They should therefore be treated as a separate summary layer, not substituted for or averaged with
 the primary PDF values.

  Important qualification
  The labels 'textbook perfect', 'optimal neurovascular symmetry' and 'excellent' exceed what these measurements can
  establish. A 0.0 C side-to-side difference does not prove normal neurovascular function, and a single static posture value
  does not determine pathology or clinical risk.

 Clinical Implications and Management Framework
 This section translates the measured pattern into a safe clinical workflow. It is not a diagnosis or treatment
 prescription.

 Clinical significance
 The overall pattern is best characterized as minor static postural asymmetry with small sagittal trunk inclination. The
 measurements do not demonstrate a major frontal-plane deviation because the relevant uncertainty intervals overlap the
 source reference. The side-specific offsets may reflect habitual stance, camera perspective, landmark localization, natural
 asymmetry or a reproducible postural strategy. Their clinical relevance depends on symptoms and repeatability.

 Thermal symmetry is neutral rather than diagnostic. Equal bilateral surface temperatures at the trapezius and lumbar regions
 reduce concern for a gross side-to-side temperature difference during this capture, but they neither exclude inflammation nor
 establish normal perfusion, nerve function or tissue health. Absolute temperatures, room conditions, acclimatization,
 emissivity, region-of-interest placement and repeatability were not supplied.

 Suggested reassessment outcomes
  • Primary: change in symptoms and task tolerance, not achievement of a single 'ideal' angle.

  • Secondary: repeatable change in trunk inclination and lateral shift exceeding expected measurement variability.

  • Supporting: side-specific offset pattern, functional tests and thermal symmetry only when acquisition conditions are
    standardized.

 Technical limitations
  • Single static video-derived observation; no direct assessment of bone alignment, joint pathology, muscle activation or
    dynamic control.

  • Several metrics are proxy measurements with no reference value; posture values cannot be interpreted as disease
    thresholds.

  • The primary report is marked draft and not clinically validated. No age, history, symptom profile, examination findings or
    validated patient-reported outcomes were provided.

  • The supplemental posture table uses a different metric convention and does not supply uncertainty or algorithm
    provenance.

  • Thermal data include bilateral differences only, without absolute temperature or acquisition protocol.

 Evidence note
 Photogrammetry can provide repeatable quantitative posture measurements when acquisition and landmarks are
 standardized, but validity varies by method and metric. Thermal measurement reliability likewise depends on equipment and
 protocol; symmetry alone is not a diagnosis.
